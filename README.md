# Beyond the Visual: Detecting Ecosystem Transitions through Soundscapes
Shenasa AS (In Prep) Beyond the Visual: Detecting Ecosystem Transitions through Soundscapes

In this study, I tested whether soundscapes can serve as reliable indicators of kelp forest condition in Sitka Sound, Alaska, by conducting paired acoustic and ecological surveys at giant kelp (Macrocystis pyrifera) forests and urchin barren habitats. This study focuses on (1) whether acoustics can enable such binary prediction, and (2) also track the continuum of ecological states along a habitat gradient. To gain mechanistic understanding of site-specific soundscape differences, I screened the soundscapes using experimentally derived acoustic signatures of sea urchin activity to: (3) determine if differences in habitat soundscapes (indicators of habitat state) were at least partially driven by sea urchin activity, and (4) assess whether acoustics could predict sea urchin abundance and activity, which could provide an “early warning” signal of transition between ecological states.
